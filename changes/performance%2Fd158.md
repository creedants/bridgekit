The mixed-separator guard now keeps its refusal when a later part of a short record reads as a formatted measure, so a foreign record such as `Oct 1, 2026;A-100;$100;$10000` under a comma header is refused instead of loading and dropping its sales.

- `src/app/numbers.ts` counts a later other-separator part as a new measure when `MEASURE_FIELD` accepts it as a plain number, or when `readsAsFormattedMeasure` finds the formatting this ticket is about (a currency symbol anywhere in the cell, a currency code at either end, grouping marks in a whole group shape, a marked bracket pair, brackets around a number, or a trailing minus) and the loader reads it as a number. `readsAsFormattedMeasure` repeats in JavaScript what `money()` and `northAmerican()` do in SQL, from the same pattern constants. `MEASURE_FIELD` and every other condition in the guard are unchanged, so `2025,A;B;C;D` under a four-column comma header and product-only punctuation with empty trailing cells still load, and `2025,X;Y;10;100` stays refused.
- A bare later part with no such formatting, such as `1e5`, `1_000` or `.5`, keeps its base decision through `MEASURE_FIELD` alone.
- A bracketed part the loader reads as text, such as `(1'000)`, does not count, so a sparse product record like `2025,A;B;(1'000);D` still loads.
- `12%` still loads, because the loader does not read a percent sign as a number.
- `test/numbers.test.ts` pins the dated-period file, the `2026,Q4;` record and its semicolon mirror through the decision, refuses the dated-period file through the real load guard, checks the guard's answer against the loader's reading for the formatted and plain forms, and loads four bracketed group forms (`(1'000)` with an ASCII apostrophe, a curly apostrophe or a non-breaking space, and `(1'000,50)`) plus the four bare forms `1e5`, `1_000`, `.5` and `100.` as short product records.
