- A price change that is exactly a band edge now counts in the band its label names: +10.0% reads under "Up 5% to 10%" and -10.0% under "Down 5% to 10%", and the same holds at 0.5%, 2% and 5%. The Price changes tab decides such a change from the exact sums of the configuration's own source money and units, so neither the division's floating-point error nor a split-row sum's rounding can push an exact edge into the next band. A change that is not exactly an edge keeps its own band even when it is only 1e-10 of a percent past one, at any price.
- `src/engine.ts`: when the Price changes tab finds a configuration within 5e-7 of a percent of an edge, `priceDistribution` reads that configuration's own rows from the source table once, sums its measure (sales, or sales minus cost) and units for each period as `DECIMAL(38,9)` from each value's shortest printed decimal, and decides the edge by comparing whole-number cross products, with an edge belonging to the band nearer no change. The cells table, the double totals, the effects and every other result are untouched. The sums are exact to nine decimal places; a value with a tenth decimal place is judged as its nine-decimal rounding, a cell whose units carry more than six decimals can sit outside the candidate window, and a cell whose values are unreadable or whose nine-decimal sums are zero keeps the previous double comparison. The labels and the bands are unchanged.
- `test/engine.test.ts`: four CSV files loaded through the same loader a person's file takes, each split across rows so the cell's double sum is inexact, cover exact edges at 5%, -2%, -5% and 10% and land in the band each label names. One test covers every edge at the edge and one cent either side. One test holds seven changes that are just past a bound, four at four-decimal prices and three at 100,000,000, and each stays in its own band. One money tie-out test holds a four- and five-decimal fixture whose whole dollars change if its inputs are rounded to three decimals: the file holds 20.49999 and 21.49998 and shows 20 and 21, while three-decimal inputs hold 20.500 and 21.500 and show 21 and 22.
